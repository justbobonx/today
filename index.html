Checking the market.

Markets are open, and stocks are drifting slightly higher while everyone waits for 2:00.

The S&P 500 opened up about 0.2% at roughly 7,736, within 1% of the record it set earlier this month. Chip stocks are doing the lifting after a blowout quarter from SK Hynix, which jumped about 10%, with Micron up near 5% alongside it. The Dow opened a hair lower. Your IVV core is along for a quiet ride until the afternoon.

The Fed is expected to hold its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4%, with futures putting less than a 10% chance on another hike today. The real event is Chairman Kevin Warsh's news conference afterward, since traders are already leaning toward a December hike. Inflation is the problem in the room, with September consumer prices up 3.7% from a year ago and wholesale prices up 6.3%. Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Dallas's Lorie Logan, and Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari are possible dissents. There is no new dot plot at this meeting.

On the Muskonomy side, Tesla closed Tuesday up 4% at about $407, its first close above $400 since July, on more merger hopes and ARK repeating its long-range robotaxi target. It's slipping about 2% this morning. SpaceX rose about 1% Tuesday to around $149, then gave back about 3% early today. No merger agreement has been filed, and both companies' special committees are still working without agreed terms.

Among other movers, Robinhood fell in late trading Tuesday after missing estimates, with the crypto slump weighing on profit.

The rest of the week stays busy. Microsoft and Alphabet report after today's close, Amazon follows Thursday, and Thursday morning also brings third quarter GDP and the September PCE inflation reading, the Fed's preferred gauge.

Turning to politics.

House Republicans are quietly preparing for life in the minority, with the midterms six days out. Speaker Mike Johnson has signaled he could stay on as GOP leader, but most of the three dozen lawmakers and aides Politico talked to doubt he would run, and they're watching Jim Jordan and Steve Scalise. Candidates have until November 6 to declare.

President Trump's record $1.5 trillion Pentagon request is running into trouble with an unlikely group, the Republican defense hawks who would normally carry it. His recent openness toward Russia, including allowing the sale of Russian diesel fuel, has soured some of them, and Rep. Don Bacon openly questioned why Congress should go along.

The year-end funding fight is the next shoe. Government money runs through December 11, and lawmakers in both parties expect another stopgap and say a shutdown is unlikely, though a bruising election could make Trump a wild card in the talks. Trump himself was in Texas Tuesday for the third time in a month, backing Ken Paxton in the Senate race and floating Venezuela as a 51st state.

Sports now.

In baseball, World Series Game 5 is tonight at 8:00 Eastern on Fox from Tropicana Field. The Brewers are giving the ball to Jacob Misiorowski on regular rest, the likely NL Cy Young winner who led the majors in ERA at 1.80 and strikeouts with 252. He threw the fastest pitch in World Series history in Game 1, at 103.5 mph.

The Rays haven't named a starter. All five of their regular rotation arms have pitched in the last four nights, so the expected plan is a bullpen game, with veteran lefty Steven Matz the most likely to cover multiple innings. Drew Rasmussen is unlikely to go on three days' rest.

In the NFL, the Colts started a sell-off ahead of the November 10 trade deadline. Eight-time Pro Bowl guard Quenton Nelson goes to Seattle for a 2027 third and a 2028 fifth, and defensive tackle DeForest Buckner returns to San Francisco for a 2027 fourth.

On the injury front, Lamar Jackson is expected back for the Ravens on Sunday in Buffalo after missing three games with a high ankle sprain. Closer to home, the Browns visit Pittsburgh on Sunday in a rematch with first place in the AFC North at stake, and Pittsburgh is a slight favorite. Denzel Ward and Grant Delpit are among the Browns' injury questions this week.

In college football, Saturday's headliner is No. 2 Ohio State at USC at 7:30 Eastern. Earlier, No. 3 Georgia meets No. 17 Florida in Atlanta at 3:30, and No. 13 Texas Tech hosts No. 22 Arizona at noon. It's the last Saturday before the first playoff committee rankings come out next Tuesday.

Something from the science desk.

Rubin Observatory has found a possible new dwarf planet beyond Pluto, provisionally named ED138, and early analysis suggests it may be one of the largest candidates ever found. It hid in plain sight near the plane of the Milky Way, where millions of background stars made it nearly impossible to spot until Rubin's software picked out the one point of light that moved.

Around the white dwarf J1328−1450, astronomers using ESO's Very Large Telescope found something new, two separate rings of planetary debris with a clean gap between them. The team thinks two survivors of the star's death keep the gap open, one a few kilometers across and the other possibly as big as Pluto or our Moon. The two rings even differ in makeup, as if they came from two different planets, or from the crust and core of one.

Here's something you might like.

India's space agency is about to try an unusual fix. Its last navigation satellite, NVS-02, was stranded in 2025 because an electrical signal never reached a one-shot valve that releases oxidizer to its engine. So for Friday's launch of NVS-03, ISRO will fire those valves open before liftoff, a first in its 51 years of launches. That removes the step that failed, but it trades it for a new question: whether the valves further downstream can hold the propellant through the shaking of the ride to orbit.
