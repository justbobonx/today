Status report, Sunday Oct 25, 2026

Good morning. A quiet Sunday so far, with a little fog still drifting off the fields. Systems are up, and your brief is ready.

A look at the weather and the day ahead.

In Central Ohio, it's foggy and cool this morning, in the upper 40s. The fog burns off into sunshine, with a high near 65°, light winds, and no rain. Clear and chilly tonight, down around 40°. Monday warms to about 71° under some clouds.

It's a full NFL Sunday, starting early with the Paris game and running through Sunday night. Baseball takes a travel day before the World Series moves to Florida.

The biggest story is the week about to start. Nine days of midterm campaigning remain, and the Fed decides on rates Wednesday, right as Microsoft and Alphabet report.

Now for the markets.

Markets are closed for the weekend, so here's a look at the week ahead.

The S&P 500 comes into the week shaky after the AI trade stumbled. Semiconductors are down more than 15% for October and memory stocks more than 20%, while the Dow is the only major index on pace for a monthly gain. Your IVV core rides the broad index, so this week's megacap reports matter more than usual.

The Fed wraps its two-day meeting with a decision Wednesday at 2:00 Eastern, then Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference. A hold at 3.75% to 4.00% is widely expected, but inflation is drifting away from target on high gas and diesel prices, so the tone of the message is the real question. Thursday at 8:30 brings Q3 GDP, September PCE inflation, and jobless claims.

Big Tech earnings are the other half of the story, with 163 S&P 500 companies reporting. Visa, Coca-Cola, and SK Hynix go Tuesday. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday after the close, Amazon and Eli Lilly on Thursday, and Exxon on Friday. After Meta's rough reception last week, investors are watching for another big jump in AI capital spending.

On the Muskonomy side, Tesla recovered from its post-earnings dip to finish last week up about 5%. SpaceX carries momentum from its strong Q3 print, and the possible SpaceX and Tesla merger talks are the thread to watch for any new detail. No Musk-company earnings are on this week's calendar.

Turning to politics.

The November 3 midterms are 9 days out. Republicans hold the House 218 to 214 and the Senate 53 to 47, so Democrats need a net gain of 4 House seats, and a Senate pickup, to flip both chambers. Polls favor Democrats for the House, and the Senate is still called too close.

Trump is swinging through Michigan to help Republican Mike Rogers in the open Senate race against Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, a state Obama also worked recently.

A new CBS News poll finds economic worries still helping Democrats, with most of their voters saying their vote is mainly to oppose Trump, though many voters on both sides don't think either party has a clear plan.

Sports now.

The 1:00 window includes Bengals at Ravens and Browns at Titans, then Packers at Lions in the late window, and Chiefs at Seahawks tonight at 8:20. The Bills, Jaguars, Chargers, and Commanders are on bye. Monday night is Cowboys at Eagles.

Puka Nacua is questionable for the Rams, and the 49ers are without Mike Evans, Deebo Samuel, and Nick Bosa. A little science.

Astronomers using data highlighted by ESO have described a Jupiter-mass object orbiting a brown dwarf called CD-35 2722 B, on a roughly 170-day orbit. Since its host is neither quite a star nor quite a planet, nobody can agree whether it counts as a moon. The team calls it an exosatellite, and the argument over labels is half the fun.

Here's something you might find interesting.

SpaceX says Starship Flight 15, provisionally no earlier than November 8, will try something new. The tower will catch both the Super Heavy booster and, for the first time, the Ship upper stage. SpaceX has also asked the FAA for up to 456 Starship launches a year from South Texas, up from the current 25. That's the kind of cadence that turns a test program into a shipping lane.
