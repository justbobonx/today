Sports now.

In baseball, World Series Game 3 is tonight at 8:00 Eastern on Fox, the first World Series game at Tropicana Field since 2008. Freddy Peralta, the ALCS MVP and a longtime Brewer before the trade that brought him to Tampa Bay, starts against Milwaukee's Robert Gasser. Peralta has been especially sharp under the Trop since joining the Rays, and the club gave him extra rest after the ALCS. Gasser works on extended rest since his last postseason start and leans on a sweeper that has been effective of late.

The Rays come home after two tight games in Milwaukee, where small mistakes and outs on the bases piled up. Tampa Bay has been strong at the dome all year, and Game 3 is the natural place to try to change the series' tone.

Monday Night Football has the Cowboys at the Eagles at 8:15 on ESPN and ABC, a 2-4 vs 2-4 NFC East scrap with the division still oddly open. Philadelphia is without Jalen Hurts, who is expected to miss the rest of the season with blood clots in his lungs, so Tanner McKee makes his second straight start after a fuller week of first-team reps. Saquon Barkley is expected back from a hamstring injury that cost him more than two games, while DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks are out. Dallas is still trying to settle a defense that has been soft against the run.

Around the league, Week 7 left a few more medical notes. Carolina tackle Taylor Moton is sidelined with a fractured fibula, and Chicago lost tight end Cole Kmet for the season to a knee injury. The Giants still sit atop a middling NFC East, which keeps Monday's matchup more relevant than the records suggest.

In college football, the new AP Top 25 has Miami still at No. 1 and Ohio State up to No. 2, with Georgia, Indiana, and Notre Dame rounding out the top five. Only a handful of teams remain unbeaten as the midweek poll reshuffles after another busy Saturday.

Something from the science desk.

Astronomers using the FAST and Arecibo radio telescopes have flagged a possible dark galaxy, a cloud of about 2 billion suns' worth of hydrogen gas with no stars anyone can detect. If it holds up, it's a galaxy that gathered all the fuel and simply never lit the stove.

Here's something you might like.

NASA's Dragonfly team ran its first full simulated flight for the rotorcraft heading to Saturn's moon Titan. The test covered autonomous flight, scanning the terrain for a safe spot, and landing, all on its own, because Earth is far too far away to fly it by joystick. Launch is no earlier than 2028, with arrival in 2034.
