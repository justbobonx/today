Status report, Tuesday Oct 27, 2026

Good morning. It's a calm Tuesday in Ohio, the kind of morning that seems to have no plans of its own. The rest of the world appears to have made enough plans for both of us.

First, the weather and the shape of the day.

In Central Ohio, it's mostly sunny today with a high near 73° and only a light east breeze. Clouds roll in tonight, with a low around 50°. Wednesday looks a touch warmer, mostly sunny with a high near 75°.

Tonight brings World Series Game 4 at 8:00 Eastern, so the recorder has another shift. Halloween is Saturday.

The biggest story this morning is the Federal Reserve, which opens its two-day meeting today ahead of Wednesday's rate decision, with Microsoft and Alphabet reporting right behind it.

Checking the market.

Markets are open, and stocks started a little higher before settling near flat.

The S&P 500 opened up about 0.3% and drifted back toward even in the first hour, after Monday's 0.7% gain ended a two-week losing streak. Oil is helping a bit, with WTI crude easing to around $101 after Iran's Supreme Leader backed the conditions his officials set for ending the war, though Brent is still near its highest level since late April. Your IVV core is mostly waiting on the Fed, like everyone else.

The Fed decision lands Wednesday at 2:00 Eastern. A hold is widely expected after September's rate hike, but traders are still pricing in more hikes by mid-2027 with inflation running hot. The 10-year Treasury yield closed Monday at 4.99%.

On the Muskonomy side, Tesla jumped 5.2% Monday to about $392 after a Barclays upgrade with a $450 target, and it's up about 2% again this morning. SpaceX slipped 0.4% Monday as investors weighed the cost of its $40B GPU lease deal, then bounced about 1% early today. Merger talk is still the backdrop, with special committees of independent directors at both companies studying a combination and no terms agreed. Musk has said he backs the idea but can't say much more without running into regulators.

Among other movers, Celestica rose about 12% before the open after beating estimates and raising its full-year outlook. Broadcom gained after arranging a $63B deal to finance chips for Anthropic, while Meta dipped after upsizing a stock sale to $25B. Coca-Cola, PayPal, and UPS all raised their forecasts, and September durable goods orders rose 1.8%.

The rest of the week is crowded. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday after the close, Amazon follows Thursday, and Thursday also brings third quarter GDP and September PCE inflation.

Turning to politics.

President Trump takes his campaign swing to Texas today, where Republican Ken Paxton is locked in a tight Senate race with Democrat James Talarico. Secretary of State Marco Rubio is expected to join him to show off a tanker of Venezuelan crude headed for Texas refineries.

The Iran war has become a real liability for Republicans a week out from the midterms. Party strategists privately say it is badly hurting their chances, with high gas prices doing most of the damage at the kitchen table.

A new NPR/PBS News/Marist poll has Trump's approval at 38% among registered voters and 40% among likely voters. Democrats lead the generic congressional ballot by 10 points with registered voters and 8 with likely voters, which points to a House gain, though the pollsters note signs that the size of any wave could be limited.

Sports now.

In baseball, World Series Game 4 is tonight at 8:00 Eastern on Fox from Tropicana Field. The Rays are starting Griffin Jax, who closed the regular season on a tear with a 1.88 ERA over his last six outings. This is only his third appearance of the postseason, and his first in this series.

The Brewers have not named a starter after reshuffling their pitching plans late Monday night. Their options include Dustin May, the only Brewer with World Series experience before this October, left-hander Kyle Harrison, who has not pitched yet this postseason, or Shane Drohan as an opener ahead of a bullpen game. Milwaukee has kept the same lineup through the series, with Jackson Chourio leading off.

In the NFL, the November 10 trade deadline is starting to shape the week. Washington lost Terry McLaurin to a torn patellar tendon and is now hunting for a receiver for Jayden Daniels, while defensive tackle Daron Payne tops the list of Commanders who could be moved. Jets receiver Garrett Wilson is another name drawing trade chatter.

Closer to home, the Browns activated Dillon Gabriel off injured reserve and plan to keep all four quarterbacks through the deadline. Shedeur Sanders stays the No. 2 behind Deshaun Watson for Sunday's trip to Pittsburgh, where both teams come in at 5-2. Sunday night also has the Eagles at the Commanders.

In college football, the playoff committee releases its first rankings of the season next Tuesday, November 3, on ESPN at 7:00 Eastern. That makes this Saturday the last week before the committee's opinion starts to count.

A look at entertainment.

A new Alien film is in the works at 20th Century Studios, with Aaron Guzikowski, the writer of Prisoners and Raised by Wolves, writing the script. Ridley Scott is producing but not directing, and no director is attached yet. It is separate from the Alien: Romulus sequel.

MGM+ ordered The Black Tapes, an eight-episode supernatural investigation series based on the 2015 podcast, with Nikolaj Coster-Waldau starring as a psychiatrist who debunks paranormal cases. Production starts in Vancouver in January 2027.

iHunt, the novel series and tabletop game about monster hunters working through a gig-economy app, is getting a horror-comedy film adaptation, with Lilly Wachowski among the executive producers and a TV pilot in development alongside it.

Something from the science desk.

TOI-561 b is a lava world so close to its star that astronomers assumed it was bare rock. Webb watched it for 37 hours and found it reflects about three-quarters of its starlight, roughly like Venus. The best explanation is clouds made of vaporized silicate, the stuff of quartz and sand, acting as sunscreen over a planet-wide magma ocean.

Astronomers have also found a gamma-ray burst whose engine kept flaring for about 27 days, about 20 days longer than the previous record. The team doesn't know what powered it. One idea is a spinning magnetar, another is a star far bigger than the usual suspects, perhaps 1,000 times the radius of the Sun.

Here's something you might like.

Japan's MMX probe is on its way to Phobos after a launch last week, on the first mission to bring back samples from a moon of Mars. It reaches Mars orbit in August 2027 and drops a small French and German rover onto Phobos. Then the main spacecraft tries two landings of its own, takes a look at Deimos, and is due home with its samples in July 2031.
